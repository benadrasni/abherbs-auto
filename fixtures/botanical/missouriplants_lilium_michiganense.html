Lilium michiganense Farw.

Michigan Lily

Family - Liliaceae

Habit - Perennial forb from a stout, scaly, light yellow bulb.

Stems - Flowering (aerial) stems ascending to erect, to 2 m, green, glabrous, glaucous.

Leaves - Typically whorled, sometimes alternate at the lowermost or uppermost nodes, to 12 cm long.

Inflorescence - Terminal whorls of 2-5 flowers (more in cultivated plants), often reduced to a single flower.

Flowers - Perianth (tepals) 6, mostly red with a yellow base and brown speckles, recurved, to 10 cm long.

Fruits - Barrel-shaped or ellipsoid capsules 3.0-4.5 cm long.

Flowering - June - July.

Habitat - Mesic bottomland to upland forests, streambanks, mesic upland to wet bottomland prairies, bluff ledges, roadsides, railroads. Frequently cultivated.

Origin - Native to the U.S.

Lookalikes - L. superbum.

Other info. - This is an easily recognized lily which is often cultivated.
